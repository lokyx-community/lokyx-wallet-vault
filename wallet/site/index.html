Direct

No exchange. No bridge.

An atomic swap, for any pair of coins. You and the other side each lock your coins behind the same secret. Either both trades go through, or both of you get your own coins back.

In the app today for LOKYX with Bitcoin and with Ethereum, on test networks, free. Other pairs are next, one chain at a time, and will show their fee before you slide.

One pair, two ways. Switch between Instant and Direct, then slide to swap.

A direct swap offer. Flip it to buy instead.

The first screen is a design preview. Instant swap, and Direct for coins beyond LOKYX, are not in the app yet.

Nobody holds your coins.

A direct swap has no exchange and no bridge to trust or to be hacked. The coins stay on their own chains.

Both sides, or neither.

If the other person stops halfway, the locks run out and each of you gets your own coins back.

Every term, in words.

Before you slide, the wallet checks the coins, the receiving address and the contract, and tells you what leaves your account.

A daily limit.
A wait on the rest.

The Ethereum vault is built from Safe and Zodiac contracts that were audited before I used them. I wrote no new contracts for it.

Spend what you need.

Payments up to your daily limit go through straight away.

Wait on anything bigger.

Larger withdrawals sit in a queue, and they can only go to your own safe address. You can cancel them from your phone before they run.

A key kept on paper.

A separate recovery key can cancel or move money, but only to your own safe address.

Rules that stay fixed.

The wait and the daily limit are set when you make the vault. Nobody can change them afterwards, not you and not a thief with your phone. To change them, make a new vault.

Cancel with one slide.

The wallet watches for withdrawals, settings changes and key rotations that you didn't start. If one shows up, you get an alert, and you can cancel it before the wait runs out.

A ring,
not a square.

Your address is drawn as a ring of lines. The coin's own logo sits in the middle, and the address is written under it with its first and last characters in bold, so a wrong one looks wrong at a glance.

Tap the ring and it flips to a QR code, so you can pay and be paid by any wallet. Bitcoin, Ethereum, Tron and LOKYX each have a ring. Solana has a wider one, with more and finer lines.

Addresses only. The app never asks for a name.

Scan anything.

A round window with rays around it reads a ring or a QR code, for any of the five coins. Pick a photo from your gallery, turn on the torch, zoom, or paste an address.

If you scan a Solana address while sending Bitcoin, the window turns red and says so before any money moves. An address you just copied is offered at the top, ready to pay.

It reads the contract
before you sign.

When a website asks you to approve something, the wallet stops and says what it is.

The three verdicts, from the app.

What it tells you.

  • What the call does, in plain words. Unlimited approvals and "approve all" are named.
  • Whether the contract, and the address getting the right to spend, have public source code.
  • Whether the contract is on a short list of audited ones. If not, it says so.
  • Unknown contracts keep the approve button locked until you tick that you read it. Approving then asks for your fingerprint, face or passcode.

Public source code means anyone can read it. It does not mean it is safe. If you are not sure, reject.

Yours, and only yours.

Keys stay on the phone.

They are made on the device and kept in its secure storage, behind your face, fingerprint or passcode.

A decoy passcode.

A second passcode opens a second wallet with its own 24 words. Nothing of your real wallet shows inside it.

A backup you can file away.

An encrypted backup file you can save anywhere. Pick it later to restore. A QR code is optional.

Look-alike addresses.

Addresses that start and end like one you know are flagged before you pay.
